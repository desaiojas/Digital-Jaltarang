What Is The Jaltarang - A Water Acoustic Instrument: 
The jaltarang is a unique percussion instrument featuring a set of ceramic or metal bowls arranged in a semicircle from largest to smallest. The instrumentalist strikes the rims of the bowls with lightweight sticks to produce melodic notes. Each bowl is filled with a specific amount of water, which tunes the bowl by altering its natural vibrating frequency. Adding water lowers the pitch because the increased mass and reduced air column slow the vibration frequency of the sound waves.

Problem Statement: 
Assembling a traditional physical jaltarang is difficult because finding bowls of the exact dimensions to build a coherent set requires rare materials. Tuning the bowls demands extensive time and either a well-trained ear for perfect pitch or dedicated reference instruments. In addition, carrying multiple fragile ceramic or metal containers filled with water makes the instrument nearly impossible to transport for regular practice and performances. This project addresses those barriers by providing an accessible, portable digital simulator.

Solution - Digitizing Jaltarang:
The Digital Jaltarang application translates this classical acoustic experience into an interactive web interface built with Streamlit. Users begin at a landing page that outlines the operational instructions and physics of the instrument. The main view presents the bowls arranged in an intuitive semicircle, mimicking the physical setup of a real jaltarang performance. Each bowl plays its authentic acoustic pitch and displays its corresponding frequency in Hertz. Instead of keyboard shortcuts, users can tune the instrument directly with dedicated interface buttons to empty the bowl, fill it halfway to lower the pitch by a half step, or fill it completely to lower the pitch by a full step. This allows beginners and musicians alike to experiment with Indian classical scales, explore acoustic physics, and play melodic tunes directly from their web browser.
